GoodHours
Initial thoughts:
The problem was to count “good” times between two given moments, where a time is good if its four digits can be split into two groups with equal products. There are three valid splits: H | HMM, HH | MM, and HHM | M. My initial thought was that brute-force iteration over all hours and minutes between the start and end times is feasible, since there are at most 24 × 60 = 1,440 minutes in a day. The challenge was correctly handling time intervals that span midnight.
Reflection:
I parsed beforeTime and afterTime into hours and minutes. If the end time is earlier than the start time, I treated it as spanning midnight and iterated through the remainder of the first day and the start of the next day separately. For each minute, I extracted its four digits and checked the three possible splits to see if the products matched.
The implementation uses nested loops over hours and minutes and simple arithmetic to extract digits and compute products. Each valid minute increments the count.
